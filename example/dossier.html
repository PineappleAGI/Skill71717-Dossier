    Put together:
    Your question can take a few shapes
    Classification
    Is X better understood as A or B? A real either/or in how the field frames it.
    Comparative / benchmarking
    Does A outperform B at doing Y? A direct head-to-head on the same outcome.
    Causal
    Does X actually cause Y? No opposing "B" needed — the tension is between studies that find an effect and those that don't.
    Descriptive / state-of-evidence
    What does the current evidence say about X? Open-ended, no adversarial pole required.
    Prevalence / scope
    How common or significant is X? Also doesn't need a "vs." — just a clear thing to measure.
    Whichever shape you use, the tool sorts evidence into supporting and contradicting based on what your question is actually asking — it doesn't require "A vs. B" phrasing.

    How this view works

    This view uses argument mapping — also called dialectical reasoning. It lays out the strongest case for and against a claim side by side, instead of forcing those arguments to converge on one answer. Some questions are genuinely contested rather than settled; the point is to see the tension clearly, not to declare a winner.
