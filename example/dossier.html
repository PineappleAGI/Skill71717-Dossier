      Briefing and what's missing

      Research Question Framework

      Lens used to structure this literature scan — not a finding of the papers themselves.

      Core inquiry intent

      Comparative / Benchmarking

      Primary lens: Sora-class video as a world simulator for planning versus generative media that only produces clips. Secondary: applied feasibility (sim-to-real) and stress-tests of lab claims.

      Epistemic rigor

      Mixed foundational + frontier

      High-citation world-model baselines (DreamerV3, UniPi, Genie) sit beside lab notes and preprints (Sora, GAIA-1, Cosmos, GameNGen).

      Scope boundary

      Video-for-planning, 2020–2026

      Machine learning, robotics, and computer vision. In: action-conditioned video, latent imagination, interactive generative environments. Out: generic world-model surveys with no video/planning tension.

      Key references

      Canonical starting points for this question.

      Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models

      Danijar Hafner, Jurgis Pasukonis, Jimmy Ba, Timothy Lillicrap · 2023

      DreamerV3 learns environment dynamics and improves behavior by imagining futures across many domains. Best academic starting point for the simulator-for-control side of this debate.

      Genie: Generative Interactive Environments

      Jake Bruce, Michael Dennis, Ashley Edwards, Jack Parker-Holder, Yuge Shi, Tim Rocktäschel · 2024

      Genie learns action-controllable virtual worlds from unlabeled videos. Widely cited when distinguishing interactive world models from one-shot generative media.

      What's missing from this list

      • Independent tests that try to prove or disprove whether Sora-class models are true physics simulators are still scarce.
      • Few apples-to-apples comparisons of pretty video quality versus success when an agent actually plans with the model.
      • Robotics transfer from foundation video world models into the real world is thinner than the demo videos suggest.
