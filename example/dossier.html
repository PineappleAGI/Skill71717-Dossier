The standard framework — PICO
Borrowed from clinical/health systematic reviews, and useful well beyond medicine.
P
Population
The subject or domain in question
"Sora-class video models"
I
Intervention
What's being examined or applied
"used as simulators for planning"
C
Comparison
Optional — a contrasting condition
"vs. generative media only"
O
Outcome
What would count as an answer
"do they function as genuine world models"
Put together: "Are Sora-class video models (P) used as simulators for planning (I) genuine world models, or just generative media (C)?"
Your question can take a few shapes
Classification
Is X better understood as A or B? A real either/or in how the field frames it.
Comparative / benchmarking
Does A outperform B at doing Y? A direct head-to-head on the same outcome.
Causal
Does X actually cause Y? No opposing "B" needed — the tension is between studies that find an effect and those that don't.
Descriptive / state-of-evidence
What does the current evidence say about X? Open-ended, no adversarial pole required.
Prevalence / scope
How common or significant is X? Also doesn't need a "vs." — just a clear thing to measure.
Whichever shape you use, the tool sorts evidence into supporting and contradicting based on what your question is actually asking — it doesn't require "A vs. B" phrasing.

Research Question Framework

Lens used to structure this literature scan — not a finding of the papers themselves.

Core inquiry intent

Comparative / Benchmarking

Primary lens: Sora-class video as a world simulator for planning versus generative media that only produces clips. Secondary: applied feasibility (sim-to-real) and stress-tests of lab claims.

Epistemic rigor

Mixed foundational + frontier

High-citation world-model baselines (DreamerV3, UniPi, Genie) sit beside lab notes and preprints (Sora, GAIA-1, Cosmos, GameNGen).

Scope boundary

Video-for-planning, 2020–2026

Machine learning, robotics, and computer vision. In: action-conditioned video, latent imagination, interactive generative environments. Out: generic world-model surveys with no video/planning tension.

Evidence supporting 9

Learning Universal Policies via Text-Guided Video Generation

Confidence 96 percent

Yilun Du, Sherry Yang, Bo Dai, Hanjun Dai, Ofir Nachum, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Dale Schuurmans, Pieter Abbeel · 2023

UniPi casts sequential decision making as text-conditioned video generation, then infers actions from the generated frames.

strong evidencepeer-reviewed method that closes video generation into control

Genie: Generative Interactive Environments

Confidence 90 percent

Jake Bruce, Michael Dennis, Ashley Edwards, Jack Parker-Holder, Yuge Shi, Tim Rocktäschel · 2024

Genie learns action-controllable virtual worlds from unlabeled Internet videos, making generative models into interactive simulators rather than one-shot clips.

strong evidenceflagship interactive generative environment from unlabeled video

Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models

Confidence 88 percent

Danijar Hafner, Jurgis Pasukonis, Jimmy Ba, Timothy Lillicrap · 2023

DreamerV3 learns environment dynamics and improves behavior by imagining future trajectories, treating generation as a planner rather than a media engine.

strong evidencecanonical world-model RL agent; latent imagination, not RGB video

AVID: Adapting Video Diffusion Models to World Models

Confidence 92 percent

Marc Rigter, Tarun Sen Gupta, Agrin Hilmkil, Chao Ma · 2024

AVID adapts pretrained video diffusion models into world models so they can be used for sequential decision-making despite scarce action labels.

moderate evidencemethod paper adapting video diffusion into world models for decisions

GAIA-1: A Generative World Model for Autonomous Driving

Confidence 91 percent

Anthony Hu, Lloyd Russell, Hudson Yeo, Zak Murez, George Fedoseev, Alex Kendall, Jamie Shotton, Gianluca Corrado · 2023

GAIA-1 generates realistic driving futures from video, text, and action so the video model can serve as a scenario simulator for autonomous driving.

moderate evidenceindustry tech report; action-conditioned driving video, proprietary data

Diffusion Models Are Real-Time Game Engines

Confidence 86 percent

Dani Valevski, Yaniv Leviathan, Moab Arar, Shlomi Fruchter · 2024

GameNGen shows a diffusion model can simulate DOOM in real time, treating generative dynamics as an interactive engine rather than a rendered clip.

moderate evidenceinteractive DOOM demo; limited domain, not real-world physics

Driving in the Occupancy World: Vision-Centric 4D Occupancy Forecasting and Planning via World Models for Autonomous Driving

Confidence 83 percent

Yu Yang, Jianbiao Mei, Yukai Ma, Siliang Du, Wenqing Chen, Yijie Qian, Yuxiang Feng, Yong Liu · 2025

The authors use a vision-centric world model to forecast 4D occupancy and plan driving, i.e. generation in the service of control rather than media.

moderate evidencedriving world model for occupancy forecasting and planning

Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform for Physical AI

Confidence 82 percent

NVIDIA · 2025

NVIDIA Cosmos presents world foundation models meant to support embodied simulation workflows, not entertainment video.

moderate evidencelab platform paper; Physical AI claim without independent physics tests

Video generation models as world simulators

weak evidenceposition note; no independent evaluation of simulator claims

Confidence 80 percent

Dan Kondratyuk, Lijun Yu, Xiuye Gu, José Lezama, Jonathan Huang, Lu Jiang · 2023

VideoPoet is a language-model recipe for zero-shot video generation, i.e. media synthesis without a planner or action-conditioned world.

moderate evidencestrong zero-shot video model with no action/planning loop

Generate Impressive Videos with Text Instructions: A Review of OpenAI Sora, Stable Diffusion, Lumiere and Comparable Models

Confidence 74 percent

Enis Karaarslan, Ömer Aydın · 2024

This review frames Sora, Stable Diffusion, and Lumiere as text-to-video generators — a media-synthesis problem — not as planners or physics engines.

moderate evidencetext-to-video systems review (Sora, Lumiere, diffusion)

Sora OpenAI's Prelude: Social Media Perspectives on Sora OpenAI and the Future of AI Video Generation

Confidence 64 percent

Reza Hadi Mogavi, D. Wang, Joseph Tu, Hilda Hadan, Sabrina Alicia Sgandurra, Pan Hui, Lennart E. Nacke · 2024

Public discussion treats Sora as a generative video tool for HCI, not as a tested world simulator for planning.

weak evidencesocial-media perception study; not a physics or planning eval

NExT-GPT: Any-to-Any Multimodal LLM

Confidence 60 percent

Shengqiong Wu, Fei, Hao, Leigang Qu, Wei Ji, Tat‐Seng Chua · 2023

NExT-GPT aims to generate content in many modalities from many inputs, which is media transformation rather than a controllable world simulator.

weak evidenceany-to-any multimodal LLM; generation without a dynamics/planning loop

Confidence 84 percent

Eloi Alonso, Adam Jelley, Vincent Micheli, Anssi Kanervisto, Amos Storkey, Tim Pearce, François Fleuret · 2024

DIAMOND argues that high-fidelity visual detail in imagined rollouts is what makes a diffusion world model useful for planning, not clip quality alone.

moderate evidenceAtari-scale diffusion world model; tests visual fidelity as a planning variable

Sora: A Review on Background, Technology, Limitations, and Opportunities of Large Vision Models

Confidence 78 percent

Yixin Liu, Kai Zhang, Yuan Li, Zhiling Yan, Chujie Gao, Ruoxi Chen, Zhengqing Yuan, Yue Huang, Hanchi Sun, Jianfeng Gao, Lifang He, Lichao Sun · 2024

This Sora review says the model can generate realistic scenes and may simulate the physical world, while stressing that public reports and reverse engineering have not yet proven that claim.

moderate evidenceSora survey of public reports; catalogs limitations rather than a new test

Verdict

9 on-topic papers support the simulator-for-planning side versus 4 against it. The weight of evidence leans supporting. 2 papers describe how you would test the claim rather than taking a side. 30 harvested items were dropped because they were off-topic or had empty abstracts.

What's missing from this list

  • Independent tests that try to prove or disprove whether Sora-class models are true physics simulators are still scarce.
  • Few apples-to-apples comparisons of pretty video quality versus success when an agent actually plans with the model.
  • Robotics transfer from foundation video world models into the real world is thinner than the demo videos suggest.

How this view works

This view uses Bayesian evidence weighting. It starts from a neutral belief (50/50) and updates that belief up or down as each piece of evidence is added.
